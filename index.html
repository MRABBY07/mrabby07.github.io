About

From the steel floor to statistical models

My path into engineering began in Bangladesh, where I earned my degree in Materials and Metallurgical Engineering at BUET and spent a year on the floor of a steel plant — running ladle furnace operations, managing molten steel chemistry, and making fast calls when things went wrong. That year taught me something that has shaped everything since: real materials rarely behave the way the textbook promises, and the gap between the model and the metal is exactly where the interesting engineering lives.

I spent the next six years turning that curiosity into a craft. Through my PhD in Mechanical Engineering at UT Arlington, fiber-reinforced polymer composites became my bread and butter — I designed, fabricated, and interrogated carbon-fiber (CFRP) and glass-fiber (GFRP) laminates from the prepreg up: laying them out, curing them, aging them, damaging them, recycling them, and measuring every property that mattered along the way. I lived in the details of these materials — how they cure, how they take up moisture, how they delaminate, and how their electrical signature quietly changes long before any crack becomes visible.

What tied all of that work together was multiphysics modeling. To predict how a composite would perform, I built coupled electro-thermo-mechanical simulations — finite element models of cure and damage and dielectric-response frameworks — and paired them with machine learning so the predictions held up against messy experimental reality. Learning to trust, validate, and quantify the error in those models is precisely the thread that carries into my current work.

Today, as a Postdoctoral Fellow at Clemson University, I focus on the question living underneath every simulation I ever ran: how much should we trust what a model tells us? I develop model validation and uncertainty quantification techniques for structural dynamics and solid mechanics — Gaussian process surrogate models with nonstationary covariance, heteroskedastic noise handling, and scalable inference for high-dimensional problems, all within the GPMSA / Kennedy–O'Hagan calibration framework. It's the same multiphysics instinct, now aimed at the confidence behind the answer rather than just the answer itself.

Research

PhD research — composite quality assessment & performance prediction

University of Texas at Arlington

Prepreg age monitoring & sustainable composites

Predictive models for aging behavior in composite prepregs using cure kinetics, dielectric spectroscopy, and data-driven ML. Correlated dipole moment evolution with cure states via molecular dynamics, and studied plasma treatment effects on out-of-life prepregs.

Cure monitoring & simulation

Multiphysics coupled simulation framework for composite cure monitoring using dielectric response, plus a post-cure quality assessment framework combining dielectric characterization with ML-based prediction.

Smart self-sensing composites

Designed and simulated piezoelectric and magnetostrictive damage-sensing composites; FEA modeling and experimental characterization using wireless detection systems for structural health monitoring.

Delamination detection & composite sorting

Framework for sorting damaged composite samples by delamination initiation, combining cohesive zone modeling in FEA with ML classification of dielectric spectroscopy data.

Composite recycling

Optimized carbon-fiber recovery from end-of-life prepregs via chemical treatment, recovering up to 85% of original tensile strength, and developed techniques to reuse recovered fibers.

Adhesive bond strength prediction

Multi-parameter approaches to predict adhesive bond strength in carbon fiber reinforced composites through surface and dielectric characterization.

Moisture content estimation

ML algorithms to forecast moisture ingress in polymer composites for lifecycle performance prediction, including coupled dielectric and moisture absorption effects.

Polymer-derived ceramics

Carbon fiber-reinforced SiC ceramic matrix composites from polycarbosilane precursors; developed sintering recipes and characterized materials via XRD/SEM/DSC. Mentored award-winning undergraduate research on electrospun SiC nanofiber composites.

Thermal interface materials (TIMs)

Studied thermal, mechanical, and dielectric degradation of silicone-based and silicone-free TIMs under prolonged thermal aging, using DMA, TMA, DSC, FTIR, and broadband dielectric spectroscopy for electronic packaging reliability.
